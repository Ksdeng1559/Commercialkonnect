Definition

What a private lender is

A private lender is a non-bank individual, investment fund, or company that originates mortgages secured by real property. Where a Schedule I bank lends from its own balance sheet against strict policy, a private lender underwrites the specific deal and prices the risk directly. That single difference explains everything else: looser qualification, faster approval, and a higher rate.

Private lenders are the standard counterparty for four situations: deals a bank declines, deals banks will not touch (raw land, construction, rural, mixed-use), deals that need to close in weeks rather than months, and short-term bridge facilities where the exit is a refinance or a sale. Most private lending in Canada is arranged by a licensed mortgage broker who places your file with a lender, so you get the shopping and the funding in one path.

Decision framework

Which source of capital fits your deal

Use this first. Most failed financings are capital-source mismatches, not underwriting failures.

Bank / Schedule I
  • ✓Income is conventional and verifiable
  • ✓Credit is 680+ with clean history
  • ✓Property is standard residential or low-risk commercial
  • ✓You can absorb a 6-12 week timeline
B-lender / chartered lender
  • ✓Bank declined on income or credit only
  • ✓Property is still conventional collateral
  • ✓You can live with prime-plus pricing
  • ✓Strong equity, some credit blemishes
Private lender
  • ✓Bank or B-lender has declined you
  • ✓Collateral is land, construction, or mixed-use
  • ✓Income is self-employment, commission, or foreign
  • ✓You need to close in under 30 days
  • ✓Short-term bridge with a defined exit
◎

Private lenders price risk directly

No policy grid means the rate is a negotiation against your specific LTV, liquidity, and completion risk. Strong equity is the single biggest lever on price.

⚖

Documentation beats polish

A coherent file beats a polished one. Prior comparables, a realistic cost-to-complete, and clean source-of-funds evidence move deals more than presentation.

⏱

Timeline is the real product

The reason to go private is speed and certainty of close. If your timeline is flexible, start with the bank — the rate differential is real.

Private mortgage lender vs private lender

"Private mortgage lender" and "private lender" describe the same counterparty. What differs is whether the lender also brokers the file. A lender that only funds is cheaper to work with; a lender-brokerage handles placement, documentation, and the BCFSA compliance obligation in one place. In BC, most private lending runs through a registered brokerage, which is why you will deal with a broker even when the money comes from a private lender.

Questions about private lenders

What is a private lender?

A private lender is a non-bank individual, investment fund, or company that originates mortgages secured by real property. Private lenders fill the gap banks leave: they accept non-conventional applications, fund in 2-6 weeks instead of 6-12, and lend against raw land, construction, mixed-use, and distressed collateral. Typical cost is 8-15% annual interest plus 1-3% lender fees.

How much do private lenders charge in Canada in 2026?

Private lenders in Canada charge between 8% and 15% annual interest in 2026, with most deals settling at 10-12%. Lender fees run 1-3% of the loan amount. Legal, appraisal, and broker fees are additional and typically total 3,000-8,000 dollars per deal.

How is a private lender different from a mortgage broker?

A mortgage broker is a licensed intermediary who shops your file across lenders and does not lend money. A private lender is the actual capital provider. Most private lending in Canada happens through a mortgage broker who places your file with a private lender, so you get both the shopping and the funding.

When should I use a private lender instead of a bank?

Use a private lender when you need funding in under 30 days, a bank or B-lender has declined you, the property is unconventional such as raw land or construction, your income is non-standard such as self-employment or commission, your credit is bruised but your equity is strong, or you need a short-term bridge for an acquisition or repositioning.

How fast can a private lender fund?

Most Canadian private lenders fund in 2-6 weeks from application. The fastest private lenders can fund in 5-10 business days for straightforward deals with strong equity. Complex construction, land assembly, or zoning-change deals typically take 3-6 weeks.

What LTV will a private lender go to?

Private lenders typically cap LTV at 65-75% on residential income property and 60-70% on commercial. Construction and land lending caps at 50-65% of completed value because of completion risk. Lower LTV consistently produces better rates.

Are private lenders regulated in British Columbia?

Yes. BC's Mortgage Services Act, in force 2026-10-13, regulates mortgage brokerages, sub-brokers, and individual mortgage brokers. Private lenders who fund but do not broker operate under lighter registration requirements, but most work through a BCFSA-registered brokerage so the transaction is documented and both sides are protected.

Do private lenders work with construction projects?

Yes, at lower LTVs of 55-65% of completed value, with progress draw schedules. Private construction lenders typically require a building permit, a fixed-price contract with a qualified general contractor, a cost-to-complete analysis, and lender-approved draw inspectors. Expect 12-15% on private construction lending.

## Positioning and core message

Commercial finance brokers can help business owners compare unsecured business credit with a facility secured by commercial real estate. Depending on the borrower, property, lender, and transaction, a real-estate-secured option may provide a lower rate, a larger facility, or more flexible terms. These outcomes are not guaranteed. The borrower should compare total cost and the consequences of pledging property, not rate alone.

Do not claim that secured credit is always cheaper, larger, or more flexible. Explain that real estate security adds collateral and enforcement risk and may involve valuation, legal, registration, appraisal, and closing costs.

## Page outline

### 1. Opening answer

In 2–3 short paragraphs explain that businesses may have multiple ways to access credit. An unsecured facility may avoid a mortgage charge on property, while a real-estate-secured facility may be considered when the business or property owner has eligible equity and wants to compare capacity, pricing, or structure. Actual terms depend on underwriting and the lender.

### 2. Quick comparison

| Consideration | Unsecured business line of credit | Real-estate-secured business financing |
|---|---|---|
| Security | Typically relies on business/owner credit support and may include guarantees or a general security agreement; confirm the actual documents. | Secured by a mortgage/charge or other registered interest against eligible real estate; exact structure varies. |
| Potential pricing | Can be higher or lower depending on risk, provider, and structure. | May be more competitively priced in some cases because property supports the facility; not guaranteed. |
| Potential facility size | Depends on business cash flow, credit, provider limits, and guarantees. | May support a larger facility where property value, equity, repayment capacity, and lender policy allow. |
| Flexibility | May offer revolving access and simpler collateral structure; terms and limits vary. | May allow a tailored term or revolving structure, subject to lender and collateral requirements. |
| Costs and process | Review setup, renewal, unused-limit, guarantee, and other fees. | May include appraisal, legal, registration, discharge, and other transaction costs. |
| Main trade-off | Less direct property collateral, but potentially tighter limits or higher cost depending on the file. | Access to property-backed credit, with property exposed if obligations are not met. |

This table is educational, not an offer or product comparison. Replace general language with product-specific terms only when there is a verified, current source and permission to publish it.

### 3. When owners may compare both

Use neutral scenarios rather than prescriptive advice:
- The business needs additional working capital for inventory, payroll, or expansion.
- An existing facility has become restrictive or expensive.
- The owner or operating company has commercial property equity that may be relevant.
- The business is considering acquisition, refinancing, or a larger capital project.
- Cash flow and property equity need to be reviewed together to understand possible structures.

### 4. Questions to ask a broker

- What is the total cost over the expected borrowing period, including fees?
- Is the facility revolving, amortizing, interest-only, or another structure?
- What security, personal guarantees, or general security registrations are required?
- What happens if the property is sold, refinanced, or transferred?
- Are there prepayment, renewal, discharge, or unused-limit fees?
- What financial statements, property documents, and business records are required?
- How quickly can the lender provide an indicative response, and what remains conditional?

### 5. Information to prepare

- Business purpose and requested amount
- Current business debt and available credit
- Recent financial statements and interim results, if available
- Receivables, inventory, and operating cash-flow information, where relevant
- Property ownership, estimated value, existing mortgage balance, and property details
- Ownership structure and relevant guarantees
- Timing and repayment plan

Do not request sensitive account credentials or full banking login details through a marketing form.

### 6. Broker role and CTA

Explain that a commercial finance broker can review the business need and property context, compare available lender structures, and explain the trade-offs. The broker cannot guarantee approval, pricing, or terms.

**Primary CTA:** Compare financing structures with a commercial finance broker  
**Secondary CTA:** Explore business working-capital options

CTA forms must show who will receive the inquiry, how the information will be used, and the applicable consent before submission.

### 7. FAQ

**Can real estate security reduce the cost of business borrowing?**  
It may in some circumstances, but the result depends on the property, borrower, lender, requested structure, and all fees. Compare total borrowing cost rather than interest rate alone.

**Can I use a commercial property to support a business line of credit?**  
Some lenders may consider property security for a business facility. Availability and structure vary, and an appraisal and legal review may be required.

**Is an unsecured line of credit safer for the property owner?**  
It may avoid a direct mortgage charge on the property, but the borrower should review all guarantees, security agreements, default provisions, and cross-default terms in the actual offer.

**Does a mortgage-secured facility always have a longer term or more flexible payments?**  
No. Terms depend on the lender and transaction. Ask for repayment, renewal, prepayment, and demand provisions in writing.

**Can a broker guarantee a lower rate or approval?**  
No. A broker can compare options available for a specific file, but only a lender can make an offer and final terms depend on underwriting.

- Do not present a single interest rate as representative without verified current evidence and all required conditions.
- Explain collateral exposure, guarantees, fees, and material trade-offs in plain language.

- Disclose sponsored placement and referral relationships where applicable.
- Keep examples hypothetical and label them as illustrative.

# CommercialKonnect — Canadian Qualified Financing Lead Marketplace Business Model

**Version:** 0.1 planning hypothesis · October 27, 2026 · CAD  
**Status:** Not launched. Pre-revenue, no measured paid lead conversion, pricing acceptance, market penetration or legal clearance. Complements [PMF evidence](PRODUCT_MARKET_FIT_EVIDENCE.md) and [CommercialKonnect PRD](COMMERCIALKONNECT_PRD.md).

## Executive model

A two-sided marketplace connecting **borrower-confirmed** commercial/business-financing needs to properly qualified financing specialists across Canada. Maintain separate products for: (1) educational directory / fixed-fee listings, (2) managed marketing and CRM services, (3) research/opportunity intelligence subscriptions (LeadSniperAI), and (4) *potential* accepted-qualified-inquiry transactions, launched **only after province/product-specific legal approval**. A discovered growth signal or public project record is **not** a qualified borrower lead.

Initial scope: BC, then Alberta and Ontario, then other provinces only after approved compliance playbooks, matching quality, and measured local supply/demand.

## Product and customer definitions

**Borrower:** business owner, owner-occupier, property investor or developer who affirmatively requests financing assistance and authorizes specified information routing.

**Buyer:** appropriately authorized commercial mortgage broker/brokerage, business-finance adviser or lender with documented specialty, jurisdiction, capacity, and permitted compensation model. Mortgage brokering, real estate services, and general commercial lending are legally distinct activities.

**Inquiry standards:** distinct person/company; explicit financing intent and requested product; jurisdiction and proposed amount/timing; valid reachable contact; freshness; no duplicate during contracted period; express/proper consent and named/understandable recipient disclosure; evidence of broker acceptance; documented dispute/credit process. Do **not** interpret intent from search signals or scrape third-party contacts into sellable inquiries.

## Market sizing — transparent scenario, not observed marketplace spend

Illustrative denominator from 2024 ISED Canadian small-employer business population **1,079,188**, using **20%** national small-business debt-financing request rate from ISED 2025 Credit Conditions Survey:

- **215,838** estimated financing-seeking businesses per year = 1,079,188 × 20%, rounded. This applies a survey rate to an employer-business count; populations may not precisely align and this is not a count of sellable leads.
- Hypothetical upper-bound *transaction volume × assumed price*, **$43.2M–$75.5M/year**, if all estimated seekers became one accepted inquiry sold at $200–$350. **Not a defensible actual lead-sales TAM:** 100% lead qualification, access, availability and monetization are implausible.
- Sensitivity based on **assumed** 1%, 3%, 5% and 10% of this demand being serviceable as one paid inquiry at $250: **2,158 / 6,475 / 10,792 / 21,584** annual inquiries and **$0.54M / $1.62M / $2.70M / $5.40M** gross national lead transaction value. These shares are hypotheses, not measured broker marketplace adoption.
- Provincial planning exposure (2024 employer counts × assumed uniform 20% financing-request proxy): Ontario 410,154 → 82,031; BC 170,512 → 34,102; Alberta 137,182 → 27,436; combined 717,848 → **143,570**. Do not describe these as verified inquiries or imply provincial financing rates are identical.
- Commercial-real-estate and development-specific applicant counts are **not independently established**. Do not add unverified categories to the national estimate; there may be substantial overlap with small businesses above.
- **TAM/SAM/SOM distinction:** underlying business financing demand ≠ legal/qualified lead transaction TAM ≠ serviceable provinces/products ≠ attainable CommercialKonnect revenue. An empirical SAM requires buying-broker counts, quote acceptance, acquisition supply and verified legal product scope; SOM requires distribution capacity and cost data.

**Sources:** [ISED Key Small Business Statistics](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/sme-research-statistics/en/key-small-business-statistics), [ISED 2025 Credit Conditions Survey](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/sme-research-statistics/en/survey-data-and-analysis/credit-conditions-survey/credit-conditions-survey-2025). Confirm population dates, survey denominators and jurisdiction splits before investor use.

## Lead tiers — pricing tests only

| Offer | Test range CAD | Acceptance proof |
|---|---:|---|
| Verified financing inquiry | $75–$150 | Actual inbound intent, verified reachability, consent, basic fit |
| Exclusive borrower-confirmed inquiry | $200–$350 | Specific need, exclusivity window, acceptance + documented evidence |
| Prequalified commercial consultation | $350–$600 | Scheduled/attended broker discussion against agreed qualification rubric |
| Development financing opportunity | Custom | Project/sponsor diligence and explicit interest; review compensation structure |

No saleable tier exists until legal authorization is reviewed. Avoid reselling the same lead contrary to exclusivity and permissions. Do not guarantee funding, conversion or lead counts.

## Revenue architecture and $50K/month scenarios

- **Predictable MRR:** professional listings ($149/month), growth listings ($399/month), and managed campaigns ($1,495+/month; services MRR only when contractually recurrent).
- **Variable transaction revenue:** accepted inquiries × realized average price, net refunds. **Never label pay-as-you-go lead transactions as subscription MRR.**
- **Production:** $595/video, $1,495 creative kit, $2,995+ developer launch (one-off revenue, excluded from MRR).
- **Intelligence:** future recurring LeadSniperAI subscriptions for verified project/growth signals; no implicit borrower authorization from signals.

Lead-only gross revenue targets, assuming all leads accepted and billable:
- 20 leads/month × $250 = **$5,000/month**;
- 30 × $250 = **$7,500/month**;
- 200 × $250 = **$50,000/month** = $600,000 annualized *transaction revenue*, not MRR.
- Example subscription allocation: 20 broker buyers × 10 accepted inquiries each × $250 = $50,000/month; only committed recurring minimum-purchase contracts might provide contractual recurring revenue, and even then record it distinctly from SaaS subscriptions.

**Unit economics:** accepted-lead contribution = realized transaction price − ads/content sourcing − verification and enrichment − compliance/privacy handling − broker routing/support − payment fees − expected credits and refunds. Example 200 × ($250−$125 fully loaded variable cost) = **$25,000 monthly contribution before fixed overhead**; entirely hypothetical. Track source-by-province CAC, accepted/qualified rate, broker speed-to-lead, show rate, rejection reasons, exclusivity, consent audit, refund rate, buyer repurchase, client concentration and gross contribution.

## Go-to-market and validation

**Phase 0 — legal and customer discovery:** province-by-province legal review for mortgage/real-estate referral activity, advertising, fixed-fee vs lead fees, licensing, compensation, privacy and CASL; 10+ buyer interviews; written lead qualification/acceptance agreements. No paid-referral launch yet.

**Phase 1 — BC concierge pilot:** three paying founding brokers for allowable directory/creative/campaign products; publish high-intent financing guides; capture explicitly consented inquiries; manually qualify/routable match and record outcomes. Goal **20–30 accepted inquiries/month** as a *test target*, not promised supply. Establish sources and a verified unit cost.

**Phase 2 — expand only if approved:** Alberta and Ontario demand/buyer cohorts after legal review, local licensing and lender acceptance. Compare qualification and cost by province; test subscription bundles and permitted accepted-inquiry pricing.

**Phase 3 — pan-Canadian scale:** standardize evidence/provenance, broker qualification, deduplication, consent routing, billing, refunds and audit; consider prepaid minimum purchase allocations after demonstrated demand. Maintain independent operations and privacy-safe analytics.

**Go/no-go:** confirmed lawful structure; reproducible borrower consent; measurable accepted inquiries and positive contribution; broker buyer repurchases; low disputes/duplicates; enough broker capacity; improving channel CAC; no dependence on speculative financing-intent inference. Do not expand based on website traffic or raw lead counts alone.

## Platform implications

- **WordPress directory/SEO:** financing problem and province-specific guides, verified broker specialty profiles, sponsored labels and explicit request-consultation forms.
- **LeadSniperAI:** company/project signals and source dates; internal prospect research and broker intelligence. Treat funding need as **unknown** until confirmed.
- **CRM / Chief of Staff:** consent history; province/product eligibility gate; match score and human approval; buyer acceptance; SLA; call/meeting outcomes; dispute/credit; attribution and contribution margin.
- **Billing:** separate subscription MRR, managed-service contracted revenue, variable accepted-inquiry sales and one-off creative revenue. Reconcile collected cash and refunds.
- **Data protection:** tenant isolation, role-based access, retention/deletion schedules, data minimization, provenance and auditable consent changes.

## Compliance hold points

Before selling any lead, obtain qualified Canadian counsel/regulatory review and brokerage sign-off for each province and product; especially BC BCFSA mortgage-services referrals and real estate lead-generation licensing, plus applicable Alberta/Ontario regulators, CASL and privacy law. Do not assume a fixed listing fee or consent alone makes referral fees lawful. Charge no deal-contingent commission absent appropriate authority. Keep marketing statistics documented, current and sourced.

## Decision

Approve **documentation and a compliance-gated pilot plan**, not an immediate national lead-resale launch. Sequence cash-generating creative/managed-service customers first; prove accepted lead supply, economics and repeated broker purchase in BC; then scale to AB/ON and Canada.

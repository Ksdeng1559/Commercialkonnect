# KonstructionComOS / ClearConnect — TAM, SAM and Product-Market Fit
Updated: October 14, 2026 | Currency: CAD | Geography: Canada, BC-first launch

## Executive decision
Launch with commercial mortgage and development-finance brokers. Use verified early construction signals to help them identify the right project sponsor, establish the financing stage, and earn a qualified conversation. Run contractor and commercial real estate team pilots alongside brokers. Use one verified project core with segment-specific qualification and outcomes.

The multi-segment opportunity is materially larger than the initial broker niche. Base-case Canadian annual subscription TAM is **$125.2 million for the expanded quantified construction and real estate services segments**, with a **$12.7 million planned BC SAM**. Adding an explicitly unverified broker planning pool produces **$129.7 million TAM / $13.3 million SAM**. Use the former figures for externally defensible discussion, with the assumptions beside them. Neither is a measured software-spending total.

Product-market fit is **unproven**. The current dashboard is a synthetic-data demo; no paid-pilot, retention or willingness-to-pay evidence was supplied. This is a fit hypothesis and validation plan, not a declaration of traction.

## 1. Product and market boundaries
The repository defines the product as a construction intelligence and capital origination platform: project discovery, stakeholder relationships, qualification, financing pipeline and source provenance. Its PRD lists brokers, contractors, developers, design firms and lenders as primary buyers. [1][2]

- Billing unit: one independent buying organization/team subscription; not one project, individual licensed broker or branch location.
- TAM: potential annual subscriptions across Canada after national coverage and segment-specific workflows exist.
- SAM: potential BC subscriptions within launch-region data coverage, conditional on the MVP being operational and relevant to those segments.
- SOM: customers that can be acquired and retained through an explicit sales capacity plan.
- The demo's currently deliverable paid-data market is not established. Planned SAM does not describe live coverage today.
- Exclude construction budgets, loan principal, investor capital, commissions, success fees and lead resale from subscription TAM.
- Exclude US expansion, suppliers, insurers and other adjacent groups from the quantified base. Developers and lenders remain strategic prospects but are not separately added without unique-account counts.

## 3. Model, assumptions and base case
Formula:
**TAM = establishments × workflow relevance × unique-buyer adjustment × annual subscription price.**
**BC SAM = BC establishments × workflow relevance × unique-buyer adjustment × launch coverage × annual subscription price.**

| Input | Base assumption | Status / reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Workflow relevance: non-residential builders | 60% | Judgment: ongoing project pursuit and fit to the planned intelligence workflow |
| Workflow relevance: specialty trades | 35% | Judgment: removes service-only, small-ticket and poorly matched project types |
| Workflow relevance: architectural/engineering services | 35% | Judgment: removes unrelated practices and late-stage opportunities |
| Workflow relevance: residential builders | 25% | Assumed share relevant to multifamily, infill and larger recurring renovation/development work |
| Workflow relevance: real estate brokerage offices | 25% | Assumed share relevant to commercial, land, investment sales and project marketing |
| Workflow relevance: activities related to real estate | 20% | Aggregate proxy for relevant property management, appraisal and related activities |
| Unique-buyer adjustment | 90% | Proxy for multiple establishments/common ownership; not observed deduplication |
| BC launch coverage | 60% | Assumed share of eligible buyers whose operating territory/project types are sufficiently covered; not a measured coverage result |
| Average annual subscription | $6,000 ($500/month) | Pricing hypothesis; not competitor pricing or demonstrated willingness to pay |

Coverage is a product/data constraint, not a sales-conversion rate. Relevance measures workflow eligibility, not willingness to purchase. Actual conversion belongs in SOM.

### Broker beachhead: separate planning hypothesis
No verified count of independent commercial/development-finance buying teams was established. All licensed mortgage professionals would be an inappropriate denominator because many focus on residential lending and share purchasing organizations.

Use **500–1,500 national teams and 50–150 BC teams** as an unverified planning envelope; base 750 national / 100 BC. These are assumed already distinct, relevant teams within serviceable territory, not a source-backed market count.

At $6,000/year:
- Broker-only national planning TAM: **$3m–$9m**, base **$4.5m**.
- Broker-only BC planning SAM: **$0.3m–$0.9m**, base **$0.6m**.
- Core plus base broker hypothesis: **$129.72m national / $13.32m BC**.

Do not present the combined figures as established market counts. Build a deduplicated list of buying teams and verify commercial activity, BC coverage and purchasing authority before using broker figures in a pitch.

Developers may already be captured as builders. Lenders/capital advisors can overlap with brokers. Allocate each eventual customer to one primary paying segment before adding either pool.

These are alternative assumption scenarios, not confidence intervals or forecasts.

| Base | 60% / 35% / 35% / 25% / 25% / 20% | 90% | 60% | $6,000 | $125.22m | $12.72m |
| Upside | 80% / 50% / 50% / 40% / 40% / 35% | 95% | 80% | $9,600 | $306.96m | $41.61m |

Adding the separate mortgage-broker planning hypotheses gives $43.30m / $129.72m / $321.36m TAM and $2.98m / $13.32m / $43.05m BC SAM. These are assumption scenarios, not confidence intervals.

The expansion adds $21.95m national TAM and $2.20m planned BC SAM versus the prior quantified core. The increase comes from added populations and eligibility assumptions, not measured demand.

The largest uncertainty is how many trades/design firms have recurring demand for this specific workflow. In the base model, specialty trades contribute about 59% of expanded core TAM; failure to earn contractor adoption materially reduces the opportunity. Holding other base inputs fixed, a 10-percentage-point decrease in trade relevance reduces national TAM by $21.20m and BC SAM by $2.22m. A 20% price reduction reduces all modeled revenue pools by 20%. Cutting BC coverage from 60% to 40% reduces expanded core BC SAM to $8.48m.

## 6. Differentiation and market evidence
ConstructConnect already markets Canadian project coverage from early planning through closeout, with plans and specifications. Early project discovery alone is therefore insufficient differentiation. [6]

Positioning hypothesis: **“Verified BC construction and real estate project intelligence that helps professionals identify the right stakeholder, act at the right stage and turn project signals into qualified business relationships.”**

A 2026 VICA summary of BCCA's survey reports late payment and procurement uncertainty among respondents. This supports investigating cash-flow and project-selection problems, but it does not establish software demand, funding intent or the ability of this product to resolve payment delays. [7]

Recommended advantages to validate:
1. Traceable local sources and dated project changes.
2. Financing-stage intelligence tied to sponsor-confirmed needs.
3. Verified stakeholder relationships with explicit uncertainty.
4. Short briefings that reduce analyst preparation time.
5. Pipeline records linking discoveries to real conversations and outcomes.

Do not promise exclusive leads, universal BC coverage, funding certainty or superior accuracy without evidence. The current mock dashboard's expandable cards and Kanban demonstrate interaction design, not these advantages.

## 7. MVP implications
Build one verified project core with three pilot workspaces: Capital Origination, Contractor Opportunities and Commercial Real Estate Relationships. These are proposed product changes, not implemented capabilities.

**First paid-pilot slice:** verified real BC project ingestion; source URL/date and freshness; sponsor/entity verification; lifecycle stage; financing status recorded as unknown until confirmed; company/project search; shortlist; persistent qualification Kanban; interaction history; export; tenant security.

Launch-region assumption: Metro Vancouver and Fraser Valley first, with other regions only as source coverage is demonstrated. The 60% model coverage factor must be replaced by measured coverage of the actual ICP, not assumed from a map.

Add data fields: segment, project source, first-seen date, last-verified date, appointment/award status, stakeholder-confidence, financing-need status, current lender status where lawfully verified, next action, opportunity origin and exclusion reason.

Keep budget, requested financing, verified funding need and committed financing separate. Do not interpret a capital pipeline move as a change to construction stage.

Delay full estimating/takeoff, credit decisions, autonomous outbound and comprehensive land-feasibility tools. They create distinct product requirements and do not follow from a useful intelligence MVP.

## 8. Ninety-day validation plan
All numbers below are proposed targets, not achieved results.

| Period | Work | Intended outcome / decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–30 | Interview 28 prospects: 6 mortgage brokers, 8 contractors across at least four specialties, 4 commercial real estate teams, 3 developers, 2 property/asset managers, 2 design/development consultants, 2 lenders and 1 appraisal practice. Review a recent real workflow and incumbent alternatives. Assemble 100 verified real projects in a deliberately bounded coverage area. | Identify the segment with the clearest repeated problem, accessible buyer and willingness to enter a paid pilot |
| Days 31–60 | Run 8 paid team pilots: 3 mortgage-broker teams, 3 contractor teams and 2 commercial real estate teams. Deliver a weekly verified shortlist plus the dashboard; record human research time and support effort. | Test whether customers act on intelligence and whether value is from software, manual service or both |
| Days 61–90 | Offer renewal at declared pricing; interview inactive and renewing customers; audit outcomes and gross margin. | Continue with the best retained segment; narrow geography or pivot if relevance/renewal is weak |

Proposed early evidence gates:
- At least 6 of 8 teams save five verified projects and create an opportunity in their first seven days, consistent with the PRD activation definition.
- At least 6 of 8 teams use the workflow in three of four pilot weeks.
- At least 5 of 8 renew at a disclosed ongoing price without exceptional discounts.
- At least half of pilot teams obtain one new, attributable, sponsor/decision-maker-confirmed qualified meeting in 30 days.
- At least 90% accuracy in a documented random sample of 50 critical project/stakeholder assertions; verify sources independently and report project versus contact accuracy separately.
- At least 80% of pilot-used records meet a seven-day verification SLA for key stage/status fields.
- Seek median preparation-time reduction of 30% against a measured within-team baseline.

These small-sample gates justify another experiment, not a public PMF claim. Review segment-specific results so aggregate retention cannot hide a failed broker, contractor or commercial real estate cohort.

A qualified broker meeting means the relevant sponsor confirms a credible project and discusses financing timing/need with an authorized participant. A contractor meeting means a relevant decision-maker confirms a genuine pursuit opportunity. A qualified commercial real estate meeting means a relevant owner/developer confirms a genuine property/project discussion with potential for a professional mandate. None of these is an approved loan, signed mandate or won contract.

Track attributable outcomes against a baseline; label assisted discoveries separately from net-new discoveries. Measure data sourcing, manual verification, enrichment and support costs. A proposed steady-state gross-margin target is at least 70%; concierge pilots may fall below it.

## 9. SOM and commercial execution
A transparent year-one base acquisition scenario:
- 250 distinct eligible buying teams reached through permitted channels/referrals.
- 40% attend discovery/demo = 100.
- 30% of attendees begin paid pilots = 30.
- 80% of pilots remain paying at year-end = 24.
- 24 × $6,000 = **$144,000 exit ARR**.

Sensitivity holding the first two stages fixed: 20% pilot conversion × 70% retention yields 14 customers / $84,000 exit ARR; 40% × 85% yields 34 / $204,000. These are capacity scenarios, not pipeline evidence or recognized year-one revenue.

The 250-team pool should include contractors and commercial real estate teams as well as mortgage brokers: it exceeds the base 100-team BC broker hypothesis. A broker-only launch must use its smaller verified team list and recalculate the funnel.

Longer-term planning: 50 retained teams = $300,000 ARR; 100 = $600,000; 150 = $900,000, at the same annual price. Expansion should follow repeatable retained usage and viable data costs.

### Revised sizing interpretation and exclusions
The added 2025 denominators are from ISED/Statistics Canada [8][9][10]. Base revenue assumptions remain $6,000/year, 90% unique-buyer adjustment and 60% BC coverage; added relevance assumptions are 25% for residential builders, 25% for brokerage offices and 20% for related real estate activities.

NAICS 5313 covers property management, appraising and listing-related activities [11]. Its revenue pool is an aggregate proxy, not separate SAMs for each professional role. NAICS 5312 includes residential resale businesses; do not label all brokerage offices as commercial prospects. The 5+ payroll screen excludes potentially valuable independent professionals and agent teams with contractor-based staffing. No separate solo-professional/seat TAM has been added.

Mortgage brokers and real estate agents are different buying categories. The former count remains an unverified planning pool; the latter uses the retrieved brokerage-office denominator. Common-parent overlap still needs account-level review.

Pure property maintenance systems, valuation engines, listing/comparable platforms and full land-feasibility underwriting are outside current MVP scope. Their customer populations enter planned SAM only to the extent the stated project-intelligence workflow is relevant and deliverable.

### One project, several professional opportunities
A verified multifamily project can support different legitimate use cases:
- Mortgage broker: sponsor-confirmed capital timing.
- General contractor: preconstruction discussion before appointment.
- Specialist trade: appropriate package pursuit when scope becomes available.
- Commercial agent: potential land, investment-sales or leasing discussion.
- Appraiser/advisor: verified research context or an appropriate assignment introduction.

Project count does not become customer count. No role is treated as available until its appointment status, relevant scope and buying need are verified.

### Revised pilot decision
The eight proposed paid pilots now compare three mortgage-broker teams, three contractor teams and two commercial real estate teams. Interview the other professions before building separate workspaces.
